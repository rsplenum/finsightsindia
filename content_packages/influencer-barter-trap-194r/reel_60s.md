That “free” PR laptop sitting on your desk could trigger a surprise ₹43,680 tax notice tomorrow morning. 

Here is the trap most creators fall into. When a brand sends you a product for review and you keep it, they log that retail value—say, ₹1,40,000—as a deductible corporate marketing expense under your PAN. 

Under Section 194R, the brand was supposed to deduct 10% TDS before sending it. But even if they forgot, the Income Tax Department didn't. 

Under Section 28(iv), any retained gift or in-kind benefit exceeding ₹20,000 in a year is treated as taxable professional income. The government’s automated AIS cross-matching system compares the brand's expense claims directly against your tax return. If you didn't report that product, the system instantly flags a discrepancy and slaps you with a 30% tax demand plus interest penalties.

If you returned the item, you are safe—as long as you saved the courier return waybills. But if you kept it, you must take action now.

Declare the Fair Market Value of all retained products under gross professional receipts in Form ITR-3 or ITR-4, and deposit the required tax using Challan ITNS 280 before filing your returns.
