## The learning loop

Three of its five mechanisms had never run. `publish_count.txt` did not exist, so the every-10-articles audit trigger fired zero times across 51 articles. H5 calibration was *unrunnable* — it needs a blinded PASS/FAIL sample and the failure log stores only FAILs. And `run_learning_loop_regression.py` hardcoded the live SKILL.md, making H3's "test a scratch copy first" literally impossible — the most likely reason the renumbering shipped untested.

Fixed: rule registry with era-scoped identity, verdict log, `audit_due()`, memory index across six stores, and `ceiling.py` — the relaxation operator, the loop's missing second move. Then the H3 gate ran for the first time, found itself broken, was fixed, and the ceiling proposal merged clean at 4/4.

**Still open:** logging coverage is 5.9%. Calibration has no sample and relaxation self-blocks below 30%. That only moves by publishing articles through the loop.

## What the process learned about itself

Two failures worth more than the fixes:

**Skipping the doctrine check.** The checks were run and posted before rung 3 (survived review); skipped before rung 4 (failed on six counts, rebuilt). Same day, same doctrine, opposite outcomes. dd-000 had *already* been strengthened once for this, by wording. Wording failed. Now `doctrineChecks.test.ts` requires a `.checks.md` per component.

**The launch gate drifted two commits.** It was the only durable store with no failure mode — everything else breaks loudly when neglected. It also lived outside the repo where nothing could see it, and cost 600 lines of hand-patched HTML to update. Now a 148-line markdown list, in the repo, with a freshness test that names the offending commits.

**The pattern:** an intention was tried first in both cases, and only the mechanism held. dd-011 now requires that any rule broken twice gets a mechanism rather than a firmer intention.

## The calculator redesign

The SWP planner went from 11 inputs before it would say anything — 8 of them requiring the expertise the visitor came for — to three questions and a sentence, with a ladder beneath it. Each rung answers one question the reader is actually having next.

The ideas each rung leaves behind: *your savings don't pay you, growth does* · *the rate you draw matters more than the amount you start with* · *a plan that only works at the top of the range is a hope* · *while drawing an income, when a fall happens matters more than whether it does*.

The sharpest single number: on the shipped defaults a one-year crash never breaks the plan, but a **two-year downturn starting in year 1 empties it in year 26** — a plan that reads 95.7% safe.
