# Search Criterion Checklist: Finding the "Gold Standard" Queries

We do not write generic dictionary definitions (e.g., "What is Section 80C?"). We mine for High-Anxiety, Symptom-Based Queries. We look for the exact friction points that cause taxpayers to panic and search Google.

Before selecting a topic to write about, it MUST pass this checklist:

## 1. The High-Anxiety Requirement
- [ ] **Is the query driven by fear or urgency?**
  Does the topic involve the risk of a Scrutiny Notice, a Penalty, a Late Fee, or losing a massive amount of money (e.g., 12.5% LTCG on a ₹1 Crore house)?
- [ ] **Does it solve a specific "Symptom"?**
  We do not write "Section 10(13A) Guide". We write "Can I pay rent to my parents to claim HRA without a notice?"

## 2. The Target Persona Fit
- [ ] **Does this directly impact one of our 5 core commercial buckets?**
  1. Capital Markets (Stocks, F&O, Crypto, Mutual Funds)
  2. Real Estate (Buying, Selling, Home Loans)
  3. The Salaried Grind (HRA, Old vs New Regime, Deductions, Allowances)
  4. Freelancers & Creators (Foreign Remittances, GST on Export, Presumptive Tax)
  5. Small Business (GST thresholds, Input Tax Credit)

## 3. The "Outcome Certainty" Test
- [ ] **Can we provide absolute certainty?**
  Will the user leave the article knowing exactly what to do? If the topic is entirely theoretical with no actionable legal "Way Out," do not write it. The article must end with a concrete, physical checklist (e.g., "File Form 10-IEA today").

## 4. The Complexity Threshold
- [ ] **Is the topic complex enough to warrant our 4-Pillar Narrative?**
  If the answer to the query is a simple "Yes" or "No" and has no Edge Cases, it belongs in an FAQ, not a Deep-Dive Article.
