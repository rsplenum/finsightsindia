Decisions — before Phase 1

Six rulings, three of which change what gets built

D-1 · Ship now, or close the blockers first?

The gate says "Not live. No domain." Indexing age and traffic history only accrue once the site is live, and AdSense needs roughly two months of both. Against that, shipping today means shipping F-01, F-02 and F-18 — small type, failing contrast, and four pages branded a different company.

Ship in week 1, but only after F-18. The trust pages are half a day and they are the one defect that actively damages you while live. Everything else improves a site that is already accruing age. Waiting three weeks to launch perfectly costs three weeks of indexing and buys very little.

D-2 · Do the four visual rules become doctrine?

Proposed, in my words, for you to accept, reject or rephrase:

  • Type has a floor. A label below the floor is not a label — it is a thing the reader's eye skips. 13px minimum for meaning, 15px for headings. (F-01)
  • Contrast is a property of the pair, and the light theme is the one strangers see. Every foreground is checked against its own ground, in both themes, before it ships. (F-02)
  • Emphasis is a budget. One all-caps element per card, one accent colour per card. Emphasis spent everywhere is emphasis nowhere. (F-03)
  • A number is a quantity, an amount and a frame — and the three are never split apart. "₹9.14 lakh more, in today's prices", never "THE SAME MONEY / ₹9.43 Lakh / in today's prices". (F-04, F-12)

Take the fourth one at minimum. It generalises the T8 frame work you already did, it is the direct cause of two separate findings, and it is the kind of rule that prevents defects rather than describing them. The other three can live as lint rules without ceremony.

D-3 · Which rupee convention wins?

Ten formatRupee() definitions and two live conventions: the answer layers' prose (₹1.28 crore) and the shared utility (₹ 1.50 Cr). Both appear on the SWP planner. Whichever you pick, the other gets deleted — so this blocks Phase 3.

The word, not the abbreviation. No space after the symbol. One decimal above a lakh: ₹1.28 crore, ₹89.1 lakh, ₹59,190. The answer layers already read as a person talking, and "Cr" is a trading-desk abbreviation that pulls the whole page back towards the terminal aesthetic we are trying to leave.

D-4 · Publish 54 articles, or hold the thin ones?

Thirty-eight sit between 1,000 and 1,500 words. Your gate already records 13 failing the R2 screen and 26 failing R7. AdSense assesses the site, not the best of it.

Hold everything failing R2 until it is rewritten. Thirty strong articles is a better application and a better site than 54 uneven ones — and the held ones lose nothing, since they are not indexed yet.

D-5 · Who is the author?

F-16 needs a real person: name, credentials, photograph, biography, @type: Person. This is yours to answer because it is your name and your exposure. It also settles whether the AI-assisted pipeline is disclosed.

A named person with a stated background, and disclose the pipeline plainly — "drafted with AI assistance, verified against primary sources by a named human, corrections logged publicly". Given you already publish a solutions ledger, that reads as integrity rather than apology, and it is far more durable than hoping nobody asks.

D-6 · Five calculators broad, or the insurance analyser deep?

The audit argued for narrowing. If you accept it, Phase 6 changes shape: the growth work concentrates on one page and the XIRR comparison table becomes the priority instead of the homepage calculator.

Narrow. But not before Phase 4 — the tax calculator is half-migrated today, and leaving it half-migrated is worse than either finishing or reverting it.

The sequence

Six phases, ordered by dependency and by risk

The ordering rule: deletion before correction, correction before construction. Phases 1 and 2 remove things and edit tokens — near-zero risk to the engines, and they shrink the surface everything later has to work against. Phase 3 changes what numbers look like, so it needs a new test first. Phase 4 builds, and only after the ground has stopped moving.

Working rhythm

How each item lands

  1. Record before acting. If the item started as Rahul's feedback, the verbatim words go into design-doctrine.json first, with do/dont arrays, then npm run doctrine. Protocol rule 1, and the one most easily skipped under momentum.
  2. Checks before component. Any new .astro in Calculators/: npm run doctrine:checks <Name>, every rule answered with a reason, before a line of markup.
  3. Test before fix, where the fix changes output. sol-055 and sol-052 both get their test written first — that is what makes them verifiable rather than merely done.
  4. One sol- id per commit, message written long. The commit carries the reasoning; the diff carries only the change. Matches the existing log.
  5. Solutions entry at the same time, not at the end of the session — including for anything deliberately deferred, with its reason.
  6. Gate updated in the same turn, then npm run gate:sync. It is a list: one line per item, tick and move on. Cap is 400 lines; it currently sits at 240.
  7. npm run verify green before moving to the next item. Build plus the full suite. A phase is not done because its items are written.

If you only do three things

The plan, compressed

Ten weeks is the honest estimate and most of it is worth doing. But the returns are steeply front-loaded, and if the next month is short, these three carry most of the value:

  1. sol-048 — rebrand the trust pages. Half a day. Unblocks AdSense entirely, and it is the one defect that gets worse the longer the site is live.
  2. sol-052 + sol-053 — contrast and type floor. Three days. This is the difference between a site your audience can read and one they cannot, and it is nearly all mechanical.
  3. sol-059 — citations. Four days. It makes the thing you are uniquely good at visible to the only three audiences that matter: readers, Google, and a reviewer.

That is roughly eight working days for the great majority of the risk retired. Phase 4 is the largest block and the one that most changes how the product feels — but it improves something already good, whereas those three stop active harm.

Deletion before correction, correction before construction. Phases 1 and 2 are almost entirely removal. That is not a compromise — it is where the return is.
