# Making the website your first source of business

You said you want this site to be your first, preferred and only source of
business. That is the right ambition, and it needs one honest correction before
anything else in this document is useful.

**A website converts demand. It does not create it.**

Nobody wakes up and types your company name. They type "chafing dish
manufacturer india", or they meet you at a fair, or a purchase manager forwards
your link to a colleague. The website's job is to take every one of those people
and turn them into an enquiry — reliably, without you being in the room.

So the plan has two halves that must run together:

1. **The converter** — the site. Built. It is designed so that a hotel purchase
   manager who lands on any page can specify, build an enquiry list and send an
   RFQ without ever speaking to you first.
2. **The channels** — nine of them below, ordered by how fast they pay back for a
   Moradabad manufacturer specifically.

Do the second half and the first half becomes what you asked for. Do only the
first and you will have a beautiful site that nobody visits.

---

## 1. Who you are actually selling to

Six buyers, and they are found in completely different places. Most exporters
fail because they market to all six the same way.

| Buyer | What they care about | Order size | Where they are | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hotel purchase manager** | Spec compliance, replacement supply in year 3, one supplier for many items | Medium, repeating | Google, LinkedIn, trade fairs, referral | 1–6 months |
| **Banquet / wedding venue** | Price, durability, looking expensive on camera | Large, seasonal | Google, IndiaMART, WhatsApp, word of mouth | 2 weeks–2 months |
| **Importer / distributor** | Margin, exclusivity, container economics, your reliability | Very large, repeating | Alibaba, trade fairs, EPCH, direct outreach | 3–12 months |
| **FF&E procurement / hotel consultant** | Meeting a designer's spec on budget and on date | Very large, project-based | LinkedIn, direct outreach, referral | 3–18 months |
| **Interior designer / architect** | Finish, bespoke capability, a supplier who says yes | Medium, high influence | Instagram, Pinterest, LinkedIn | 2–9 months |
| **Individual retail buyer** | The object, the story, trust | Small | Instagram, Google, marketplaces | Same day |

The most valuable of these is the fourth — **hospitality procurement
consultants and FF&E firms**. One relationship there can put you on a
multi-property specification. Almost no Moradabad manufacturer courts them,
because they are not on the marketplaces everyone else fights over.

## 2. Nine channels, in the order I would actually do them

### Channel 1 — Google, organic (the foundation) · Cost: your time · First results: 3–6 months

This is already built. The site ships with **92 programmatic landing pages** —
pages like `/chafing-dish-manufacturer`, `/wine-cooler-exporter`,
`/charger-plate-supplier-in-dubai`, `/chafing-dishes-for-banquet-halls` — each
targeting a real search a buyer makes, each with its own specification content,
FAQ schema and product list.

That is the machinery. It needs three things from you to actually rank:

1. **The real product photographs.** Google now reads images heavily for
   commercial queries, and a page of placeholders will not compete.
2. **Google Business Profile.** Free, fifteen minutes, and it is how you appear
   for "metalware manufacturer near me" and in Google Maps. Add the factory
   address, the categories, photographs of the floor, and your hours. Ask every
   satisfied buyer for a review there. For a manufacturer with a physical
   address, this is the single highest-return free thing available.
3. **Search Console.** Verify the site, submit the sitemap, then check it monthly
   to see which of the 92 pages are getting impressions. The ones that get
   impressions but no clicks need a better title. The ones getting clicks tell
   you what to build more of.

**Then keep writing.** Three journal guides ship with the site. Add one a month.
Topics that work for you specifically: "how many chafing dishes per 100 guests",
"304 vs 202 steel for hotels", "what a charger plate should weigh", "chafing dish
price in India". Write them the way you would explain it on the phone. That voice
is your advantage — a trading house cannot fake it.

### Channel 2 — B2B marketplaces · Cost: ₹40k–₹1.5L/yr · First results: 2–6 weeks

Uncomfortable but true: **IndiaMART will bring you enquiries faster than your own
website will.** Not better enquiries — faster ones.

- **IndiaMART** — the default for Indian buyers. A paid listing generates enquiry
  volume within weeks. Treat it as a lead source, not a shopfront.
- **Alibaba Gold Supplier** — the same for international buyers. Expensive, and
  you compete against Chinese manufacturers on price, which you will lose. You
  win on hand-hammered work, brass and copper craft, and low MOQs, so lead with
  those.
- **TradeIndia / ExportersIndia** — cheaper, lower quality, worth having.

**The critical move:** every marketplace listing should link to your own site. The
marketplace gives you the enquiry; your site is what makes them believe you are a
factory rather than the fourth identical listing they opened. Use the
marketplaces to buy attention and your site to convert it. Over two or three
years, shift the balance as your organic traffic grows.

### Channel 3 — Trade fairs · Cost: ₹2–8L per fair · First results: immediate, compounding

For an exporter, this is still the highest-conversion channel that exists. A
buyer who has held your chafing dish does not need convincing about the gauge.

| Fair | Where | Who attends | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IHGF Delhi Fair** | Greater Noida, twice yearly | Global handicraft importers | The obvious first one. EPCH organises it, you are four hours away, and subsidised stalls are available to members. |
| **AAHAR** | Delhi, March | Indian hotels, HORECA | Best route to Indian hotel chains in one room. |
| **Ambiente** | Frankfurt, February | European importers, retail | The big one for Europe. Expensive. Go after IHGF has proved the pitch. |
| **Gulfood / HORECA** | Dubai | Gulf hotels — your biggest export market | High-return for hospitality metalware specifically. |
| **HOSTMILANO** | Milan, odd years | European hospitality supply | Very high quality, very competitive. |

**Do this properly or not at all.** Book a stall, take physical samples of your
best six pieces in three finishes, take a QR code to the site on every surface,
and collect cards obsessively. Then — this is the part almost everyone skips —
**follow up within 48 hours** with a personal email referencing what you
discussed. Most fair leads die of neglect, not disinterest.

EPCH membership gets you subsidised participation, market intelligence and buyer
delegations. If you are not a member, join. It is the cheapest thing on this list.

### Channel 4 — Direct outreach to hotels · Cost: your time · First results: 4–12 weeks

Nobody in Moradabad does this well, which is why it works.

**The target:** Purchase Manager, Materials Manager, F&B Director, or for a new
property, the Pre-Opening Team. Find them on LinkedIn — most Indian and Gulf
hotels list them.

**The trigger that makes this work:** new property openings and refurbishments. A
hotel opening in eight months needs everything, has a budget, and has not chosen
a supplier. Watch HVS and Hotelivate reports, hospitality trade press, and
LinkedIn announcements. A property announcing a 2027 opening is a live
opportunity today.

**The message that works** is short, specific and offers something before it asks:

> Subject: Chafing dishes for the [Property] pre-opening
>
> Mr/Ms [Name] — I make hotel metalware in Moradabad. Thirty years, my own
> factory. I saw [Property] opens in [Month].
>
> If it is useful, I will send you our specification sheet for buffet lines — how
> many chafers per hundred covers, which steel grade for a pool deck, what a
> charger plate should weigh. No cost and no obligation; it is what I would tell
> you on the phone.
>
> If you would rather see the factory first, I will walk you around it on a video
> call whenever suits you.
>
> Rajendra Sharma, Tripadi International
> [link to the factory page]

Twenty of these a week, sent by hand, will outperform any amount of advertising.
The video-call offer is your unfair advantage: a trading house cannot accept it.

### Channel 5 — FF&E and hospitality procurement firms · Cost: your time · First results: 3–9 months, then very large

The channel nobody works. Firms that specify and buy on behalf of hotel groups —
procurement consultancies, hotel FF&E specialists, pre-opening consultants.

One accepted supplier registration can put you on the specification list for
every property that firm touches. The cycle is long and the paperwork is real
(they will want certifications, insurance, references, a capability statement).
Start the conversations now so that in eighteen months you are on the list.

### Channel 6 — Instagram and Pinterest · Cost: your time · First results: 2–4 months

Your products are photogenic and your factory is more so.

**What actually performs, in order:**

1. **Process video.** A copper sheet becoming a platter. Hammering. The moment
   something comes out of the plating bath. Thirty seconds, no music-video
   editing, no captions needed. This material travels further than any product
   shot and cannot be copied by a competitor who does not own a factory.
2. **The finished piece in a room.** Not on white — on a table, on a buffet, in
   use. This is what a designer saves and sends to a client.
3. **Before and after.** Raw sheet, finished piece, side by side.
4. **Your hands.** Thirty years of them. People buy from people.

**Pinterest matters more than it looks for your category.** Wedding planners,
event designers and interior designers use it as a working tool, and pins have a
life measured in years rather than hours. Every product photograph should be
pinned with a keyword-rich description linking to the product page.

Post three times a week. One person with a phone can do it. Consistency beats
production value by a wide margin here.

### Channel 7 — YouTube: the moat · Cost: your time · First results: 6–12 months, then permanent

Longer, harder, and the one thing on this list that no competitor can take from
you.

A series of five-minute videos filmed on the factory floor: *"How a chafing dish
is actually made"*, *"Why your charger plates went yellow"*, *"304 vs 202: what a
magnet does and does not tell you"*, *"What to check before you pay a Moradabad
supplier"*.

You are not selling in these. You are being the person who knows. Buyers who
watch you explain a hinge for four minutes arrive at your enquiry form already
convinced. It also feeds Channel 1, because Google increasingly surfaces video
for commercial research queries.

You do not need production. A phone on a tripod, good light from the shed door,
and you talking the way you would talk to a buyer standing next to you.

### Channel 8 — Google Ads, targeted · Cost: ₹20–60k/month · First results: days

Do this **after** the photographs are in, not before — you would be paying to
send people to placeholders.

Then run it narrowly. Do not bid on "chafing dish". Bid on:
- `chafing dish manufacturer india`
- `charger plates bulk supplier`
- `hotel buffet equipment wholesale`
- `copper serveware manufacturer`
- `brass chafing dish exporter`

Long, specific, commercial-intent phrases. Send each ad to the matching pSEO
page, not the homepage — the pages already exist for exactly this. Then measure
cost per enquiry, kill anything above your threshold, and double what works.

### Channel 9 — Retail marketplaces · Cost: commission · First results: 2–6 weeks

For the retail half: Amazon India, Flipkart, and — for copper and brass
serveware specifically — Etsy, which pays well for handmade craft with a story.

Retail is not where your money is. It is where your **proof** is: reviews,
photographs of your pieces in real homes, and a cash flow that does not depend on
a single container. Treat it as marketing that pays for itself.

## 3. Turning browsers into buyers

Traffic that does not convert is a hobby. Here is what the site already does, and
what to add.

### Built in

- **The enquiry list.** A buyer collects pieces across the site with quantities
  and sends one RFQ. It persists in their browser between visits — which matters,
  because a purchase manager rarely finishes a specification in one sitting. This
  is the single most important conversion mechanic on the site: it converts
  browsing into a specification document without a conversation.
- **WhatsApp everywhere.** For Indian and Gulf buyers, WhatsApp is the default
  business channel. Every product page has a pre-filled WhatsApp link naming that
  exact piece.
- **A form that never dead-ends.** If the mail endpoint fails, it composes the
  message into email and WhatsApp instead. A filled-in enquiry is never lost.
- **"In use" pages.** Seven working settings — banquet, fine dining, poolside,
  room service, bar, breakfast, home — because a photograph of an object tells a
  buyer what it is and a photograph of a room tells them whether they need it.
  This is the bridge you asked for between inspiration and purchase.
- **Objections answered on the page.** MOQ, lead time, branding, HS code, care —
  on every product page. Every question you answer on the page is a buyer who
  does not leave to find the answer somewhere else.
- **The factory as the argument.** "You are buying from the man who makes it"
  runs through the whole site, because it is true and because your competitors
  cannot say it.

### What to add, in order of return

1. **A downloadable PDF catalogue behind an email.** The page exists; it needs
   the PDF. Purchase committees pass PDFs around. It is also the cleanest way to
   build a list of buyers who are actively specifying.
2. **Case studies.** "300-cover wedding at [venue] — what they specified and
   why." Three of these are worth more than thirty product pages.
3. **Named clients or logos.** One recognisable hotel name changes the site's
   credibility more than any redesign.
4. **A sample offer, stated plainly.** "Samples in 7–10 days, cost credited
   against your first order." This is a real risk-reversal and it belongs on the
   wholesale page as a stated promise.
5. **A live quote turnaround you can actually keep.** The site promises one
   working day. Keep it, or change it. Breaking it is worse than not promising.
6. **The unscheduled video call.** Currently a line in the journal. It should be
   a standing offer with a button: *"Call us from the factory floor, unannounced,
   whenever you like."* No trading house can match it, and it settles the single
   biggest doubt an overseas buyer has.

### The follow-up that most manufacturers never do

Of every ten enquiries you receive, perhaps two reply to your first quote. The
other eight are not uninterested — they are busy.

A three-touch follow-up (day 3, day 10, day 30) roughly doubles conversion. It
does not need software. It needs a spreadsheet with a date column and someone who
checks it every morning.

- Ask your first two customers for a testimonial and a Google review

## 5. What to measure

Four numbers, monthly. Everything else is noise.

1. **Enquiries received.** The only number that matters. Everything else is a
   means to it.
2. **Where each one came from.** Ask on the form — it is already a field. Without
   this you cannot tell which channel to double.
3. **Quote-to-order conversion.** If you send twenty quotes and win one, the
   problem is the quote or the follow-up, not the traffic.
4. **Organic impressions in Search Console.** Slow to move, and the leading
   indicator that the 92 pSEO pages are working.

## 6. Four things not to do

1. **Do not buy backlinks or hire an "SEO package" that promises rankings.** It
   works for a quarter and then Google removes you. The pSEO pages plus real
   content is the durable version of the same thing.
2. **Do not compete on price with Chinese manufacturers.** You will lose, and you
   will train buyers to see you as a commodity. Compete on hand-work, brass and
   copper craft, low MOQs, bespoke tooling and the fact that you answer the phone
   yourself.
3. **Do not claim a certification you do not hold.** One buyer asks for the
   certificate and the relationship is over. "We do not hold ISO 9001 but you are
   welcome to audit us or send your own inspector" is a stronger answer than a
   false claim, and it is a genuinely persuasive one.
4. **Do not let the website be the last thing you update.** A price list from
   2024 and a product that is no longer made will cost you more than the site
   earns. Fifteen minutes a month.

## 7. The honest summary

The website is now a genuinely good converter. It is better than almost anything
in your category, because it argues from the factory floor rather than from
adjectives.

It will not, on its own, be your only source of business — not in the first year.
What it will do is make every other channel work several times harder. The
buyer who meets you at IHGF, the one who finds you on IndiaMART, the one you
email cold: all three end up on this site, and what they find there decides
whether they enquire.

Build the channels. The site will convert them.

And the two things that would move the needle fastest, today: **send the
photographs**, and **start filming the factory floor**. Everything else is
downstream of those.
