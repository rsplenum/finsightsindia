## Part 2: The 51 Core Forensic Topics

### Direct Tax & Personal Finance
18. **The Form 26AS/AIS Blindspot Penalty:** Relying only on Form 16 and ignoring the AIS, leading to mismatched dividend/interest reporting and a definitive Section 143(1) intimation.
19. **Section 80D Preventive Health Checkup Rejection:** Claiming ₹5,000 in cash without diagnostic receipts, triggering automated disallowances.
20. **The Capital Gains Tax-Loss Harvesting Wash Sale:** Harvesting losses on March 31st and buying back on April 1st, triggering Assessing Officer scrutiny under General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR).
21. **The Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) Partition Trap:** Dissolving an HUF incorrectly without a formal partition deed, leading toclubbing of all HUF income back to the Karta.
22. **The Senior Citizen FD Interest Mismatch (Section 80TTB):** Claiming 80TTA instead of 80TTB, resulting in a ₹40,000 lost deduction due to a simple dropdown error.
23. **The Section 87A Rebate Illusion in Special Rates:** Assuming ₹7 Lakhs is completely tax-free under the New Regime, but realizing Short Term Capital Gains (111A) are not fully covered by the rebate.
24. **The Agricultural Income 10(1) Audit Bait:** Showing massive agricultural income to bypass tax, instantly triggering severe scrutiny and demands for land records, APMC receipts, and transport bills.
25. **The Post-Office Scheme Interest Ambush:** Forgetting to declare NSC or KVP accrued interest yearly, resulting in a massive tax bracket bump upon maturity.
26. **Section 54EC Bond Allocation Miss:** Missing the strict 6-month deadline from the date of property sale to invest in NHAI/REC bonds, vaporizing the ₹50 Lakh capital gains exemption.
27. **Dream11 and Lottery Winnings (Section 115BBJ):** The assumption that platform TDS covers your liability, missing the surcharge and cess on massive winnings.
28. **The "Gift to Minor" Clubbing Boomerang (Section 64):** Gifting shares to a minor child, only to have the massive dividends clubbed into the parent's highest tax bracket.
29. **The Unlisted Shares FMV Trap (Section 56(2)(x)):** Buying unlisted shares (startups) below Fair Market Value and paying "Income from Other Sources" tax on the difference.
30. **Section 54 vs 54F Mix-up on Capital Gains:** Reinvesting proceeds from commercial property into residential under Section 54 instead of 54F, resulting in total disallowance of the exemption.
31. **The Education Loan Interest (Section 80E) Timeline:** Missing the 8-year absolute window for claiming 80E deductions on massive foreign education loans.
32. **Reverse Mortgage Loan (RML) Misconceptions:** Seniors hiding RML payouts from ITR under fear of taxation, when payouts are actually tax-exempt under Section 10(43).

### Corporate & Startup Audits
33. **Angel Tax Nuances (Section 56(2)(viib)):** Small founders issuing shares at a premium without a strict merchant banker valuation, triggering the draconian Angel Tax (though recently abolished, legacy audits remain).
34. **Section 43B Provident Fund Delay:** Depositing employee PF on the 16th instead of the 15th, making the entire amount a permanently disallowed expense for the company.
35. **The Director's Remuneration vs Dividend Trap:** Taking a massive dividend instead of a salary to avoid PF, triggering Section 2(22)(e) deemed dividend audits.
36. **Corporate Guarantees by Directors:** Directors giving personal guarantees for company loans, potentially triggering deemed GST implications or transfer pricing scrutiny.
37. **Section 194R (Business Gifts & Influencer Perks):** Gifting laptops or trips to distributors/influencers without deducting 10% TDS, resulting in total disallowance of the marketing expense.
38. **The Section 80JJAA Employment Generation Claim Rejection:** Failing to get the exact CA certification (Form 10DA) to claim 30% deduction on new employee wages, losing massive corporate tax savings.
39. **The Deemed Dividend Trap (Section 2(22)(e)):** A founder taking a "loan" from their own highly profitable private limited company, triggering a flat 30% tax as a deemed dividend.
40. **TDS on Software vs Professional Fees:** Deducting 2% (194J-Tech) instead of 10% (194J-Prof) on a consulting invoice, leading to a "TDS Short Deduction" demand with interest.
41. **The Co-Working Space TDS Paradox (Section 194-I):** Deducting 10% on "rent" instead of 2% on "services" for WeWork invoices, causing vendor disputes and TDS mismatch.

### Goods and Services Tax (GST)
42. **The Input Tax Credit (ITC) Rule 36(4) Reversal:** Claiming ITC because you have the invoice, but the vendor hasn't filed GSTR-1. Result: You pay the vendor's GST out of pocket + 18% interest.
43. **The Inter-State Branch Transfer (Cross-Charge) Trap:** Sending services (like HR or IT) from the head office to a branch in another state without raising a GST invoice, triggering evasion notices.
44. **The Composition Scheme Turnover Breach:** A small retailer crossing ₹1.5 Crores mid-year, failing to switch to regular GST, and being hit with retrospective 18% GST on all sales.
45. **The Advance Receipt GST Nightmare:** Taking an advance payment for services on March 30th, not raising the invoice, and failing to pay GST on the advance in the same month.
46. **The Cancelled Registration Revival Deadlock:** Missing the 30-day window to revoke a cancelled GST number, effectively killing the business's ability to trade B2B legally.
47. **GST on Director's Sitting Fees (RCM):** Failing to pay 18% Reverse Charge GST on the fees paid to independent directors.
48. **Export of Services vs Intermediary Trap:** A freelancer acting as a middleman for a US client. The IT Dept classifies them as an "Intermediary," destroying their LUT zero-rating and demanding 18% IGST.
49. **The Free Sample ITC Block (Section 17(5)(h)):** Giving away free samples of FMCG goods and forgetting to reverse the Input Tax Credit on those specific goods.
50. **The Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) GST Ban:** Attempting to claim ITC on mandatory CSR expenses, which is expressly disallowed by advance rulings.
51. **The Notice Pay Recovery GST Trap:** Deducting 1-month salary from an absconding employee and failing to pay 18% GST on that "Notice Pay Recovery."

### NRIs, Forex & International Tax
52. **The Double Taxation (DTAA) TRC Rejection:** Trying to claim DTAA relief without a valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from the foreign country, resulting in maximum marginal rate taxation in India.
53. **The PF Withdrawal TDS for NRIs:** Withdrawing Indian EPF while a resident of the US, suffering massive 30% TDS, and struggling to claim foreign tax credits in the US.
54. **Taxation of Foreign Trusts (Beneficial Ownership):** An NRI setting up a trust in Dubai but exercising control from India, triggering "Place of Effective Management (POEM)" rules and taxing the trust globally in India.
55. **The ESOP Vesting vs Exercise Forex Trap:** Paying Indian tax upon exercising a US tech company's ESOPs, but miscalculating the SBI TT Buying Rate on the day of exercise, leading to a massive shortfall.
56. **Inheriting Foreign Assets:** A Resident Indian inheriting an apartment in London from an NRI uncle and failing to immediately declare it in Schedule FA of the ITR.
57. **The LRS Outward Remittance 20% TCS Trap:** Sending ₹10 Lakhs abroad for investment, getting hit with 20% TCS, and the massive cash flow blockage it creates until ITR filing.
58. **Repatriation Limits (NRO to NRE):** The absolute $1 Million limit per financial year for transferring funds from an NRO to an NRE account and the strict 15CB CA certificate required.

60. **The Crypto Wallet Transfer Tax:** Transferring crypto from Binance to a Ledger hardware wallet, and the ambiguity around triggering a taxable event.

64. **AIF (Alternative Investment Fund) Pass-Through Taxation:** HNIs failing to report Category III AIF income correctly because it does not enjoy the pass-through status of Categories I & II.
