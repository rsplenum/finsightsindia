# Hitting the 30% Wall

{/* Image: Varsity style illustration of a successful freelancer looking at a roadmap showing a transition from a tiny solopreneur desk to a large agency office, with massive tax savings bags along the path. Pure white background, crisp minimal lines, high quality vector art style. */}
![Agency Arbitrage Roadmap](../../assets/images/agency-arbitrage.jpg)

> *"My freelance UI/UX design income just crossed ₹80 Lakhs this year. For years, I enjoyed paying almost nothing under the Section 44ADA presumptive scheme. But now my CA says I'm forced into the 30% individual tax slab, I have to maintain books of accounts, and I'll lose ₹20 Lakhs to taxes! How do I scale without the government punishing me?"*

Section 44ADA is the holy grail for Indian freelancers. It allows you to declare 50% of your gross receipts as pure profit, effectively halving your taxable income and shielding you from the grueling requirement of maintaining books of accounts.

But the government didn't design Section 44ADA for empire builders. They capped it at **₹50 Lakhs**, and recently expanded it to **₹75 Lakhs** (provided 95% of your receipts are digital). 

The moment you earn ₹75,00,001, the shield shatters.

## 1. The ₹75 Lakh Trap

When you cross the Section 44ADA threshold, you are immediately dumped into the standard individual taxation framework. Here is what happens:

1. **Mandatory Bookkeeping (Section 44AA):** You can no longer just declare 50% profit. You must maintain rigorous books of accounts, tracking every single business expense, invoice, and receipt.
2. **Mandatory Tax Audit (Section 44AB):** Because your gross receipts exceed the professional limit without presumptive protection, you must hire a Chartered Accountant to formally audit your books and file a Tax Audit Report.
3. **The 30% Slab:** Your net profit is now taxed at your individual slab rate. Since your profit is likely very high, the vast majority of your income will be taxed at the peak **30% rate** (plus a 4% health & education cess). 
4. **The Surcharge:** If your net taxable income crosses ₹50 Lakhs, a 10% surcharge kicks in, pushing your effective tax rate to nearly **34.32%**.

Scaling your freelance business just triggered the most punitive tax brackets in India. You are essentially being punished for growing.

## 2. The Solution: Agency Arbitrage via Section 115BAA

To survive the ₹75L threshold, you must stop operating as an "Individual" and start operating as an "Agency." 

The Indian government taxes high-earning individuals brutally, but it rewards corporations with massive tax cuts. By registering a **Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd)**, you can instantly arbitrage the tax code to your advantage using **Section 115BAA**.

### The 25% Corporate Flat Rate
Under Section 115BAA (introduced via the Taxation Laws Amendment Act, 2019), domestic companies can opt for a heavily concessional flat tax rate.

If your Pvt Ltd company foregoes certain specific deductions (like SEZ holidays or additional depreciation), your corporate tax rate drops to **22%**. 

When you add the mandatory 10% surcharge and 4% cess, the effective, final corporate tax rate is **25.168%**.

Compare this to the 34.32% effective rate you would pay as an individual crossing the surcharge limits. You instantly save nearly 9% on every rupee of profit, simply by changing the legal wrapper around your business.

## 3. The 194J vs 194C Subcontracting Loophole

As you scale from a freelancer to an agency, you cannot do all the work yourself. You must hire other freelancers, developers, or designers to execute the projects. This opens up the **Subcontracting Arbitrage**.

<NoticeTrap title="The 10% vs 2% TDS Mismatch">
When a foreign client or a large Indian corporation pays *you* (the agency) for software development or consulting, they deduct **10% TDS under Section 194J** (Professional/Technical Services).

However, when *you* subcontract the execution of that project to a junior freelancer or a vendor team, you do not necessarily have to deduct 10%. If the work is a standard, defined contract (like bulk data entry, basic video editing, or standard coding against a fixed spec), you can often classify the payment to your sub-vendor under **Section 194C (Payments to Contractors)**, which attracts only **1% or 2% TDS**.
</NoticeTrap>

This creates a massive cash flow advantage. You are receiving money with a 10% TDS credit (which you can claim back as a refund later), but you are paying out money to your team while only withholding 1% or 2%. Your working capital remains highly liquid inside the company.

## 4. The Founder's Salary Strategy

The real magic of the Private Limited structure is the ability to split your income between two separate legal entities: **The Company** and **You (The Employee)**.

As the Director of your Pvt Ltd company, you can pay yourself a salary. This salary is a **100% tax-deductible expense** for the company, lowering its taxable profit.

### The Ultimate Arbitrage Math
Assume your agency generates **₹1.5 Crores** in revenue, and your only real expense is your own time.

**Scenario A: Operating as an Individual (No Presumptive Scheme)**
- Net Profit: ₹1.5 Crores.
- Tax Slab: 30% + 15% Surcharge (since income > ₹1Cr) + 4% Cess.
- Effective Tax Rate: **35.88%**.
- Total Tax Paid: **~₹52 Lakhs**.

**Scenario B: Operating as a Pvt Ltd Company**
You decide to pay yourself a Founder's Salary of **₹15 Lakhs**, leaving ₹1.35 Crores in the company.

1. **Your Individual Tax:** The ₹15 Lakhs salary falls entirely into the lower tax slabs (especially under the New Regime). You pay roughly **₹1.5 Lakhs** in personal tax.
2. **The Company's Tax:** The company's taxable profit is reduced to ₹1.35 Crores. Taxed at the flat 25.168% rate under Section 115BAA. The company pays **₹33.9 Lakhs** in corporate tax.
3. **Total Combined Tax Paid:** ₹1.5L + ₹33.9L = **~₹35.4 Lakhs**.

By simply wrapping your freelance operation in a Pvt Ltd company and paying yourself a modest salary, you saved **₹16.6 Lakhs in taxes in a single year**.

## 5. The 360-Degree Edge Cases & Warnings

While this arbitrage is incredibly powerful, you must navigate the edge cases carefully:

### Edge Case 1: The Dividend Double-Tax Trap
How do you get the ₹1.35 Crores *out* of the company and into your personal bank account? 
If you declare it as a dividend, the company pays the money out, but **you** are taxed on that dividend at your personal slab rate (which will be 30%!). This effectively double-taxes the money (25% at the corporate level + 30% at the personal level). 
**The Solution:** Do not pull the money out as dividends. Keep the wealth inside the company. Use the company to invest in mutual funds, buy commercial real estate for your office, or buy equipment. The company becomes your wealth-building vehicle.

### Edge Case 2: Section 40A(2)(b) - Unreasonable Salaries
"What if I pay my spouse a ₹20 Lakh salary to lower the corporate profit, even though they don't work for the agency?"
The Assessing Officer will invoke **Section 40A(2)(b)**, which allows them to disallow expenses paid to "specified persons" (relatives) if the amount is unreasonable or excessive for the fair market value of the services rendered. If your spouse isn't doing ₹20 Lakhs worth of work, the expense is disallowed, and the company owes 25% tax on it anyway.

### Edge Case 3: Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs)
What about an LLP? LLPs do not qualify for the 25.168% flat rate under Section 115BAA. LLPs are taxed at a flat **30%** (+12% surcharge if income > ₹1Cr). While LLPs are easier to maintain compliance-wise, they completely destroy the 25% tax arbitrage of a Pvt Ltd company. If your goal is to minimize tax while scaling, the Pvt Ltd is the superior vehicle.

## 💡 Key Takeaway Summary & Actionable Checklist

*   **Monitor the ₹75L Threshold:** The moment your digital gross receipts cross ₹75 Lakhs in a financial year, Section 44ADA presumptive taxation is gone. Prepare for mandatory bookkeeping and tax audits.
*   **Incorporate a Pvt Ltd Company:** To escape the punitive 35%+ individual surcharge rates, incorporate a company and opt for the Section 115BAA 25.168% flat corporate tax rate.
*   **Leverage Founder's Salary:** Deduct a reasonable salary for yourself to utilize the lower individual tax slabs under the New Regime, while leaving the bulk of the profit in the 25% corporate bucket.
*   **Arbitrage Subcontractors:** Classify sub-vendor payments correctly under Section 194C (1%-2%) rather than defaulting to Section 194J (10%) to maintain massive working capital liquidity.
*   **Never Pay Dividends:** Keep the post-tax profit inside the company and invest it through the corporate entity to avoid the devastating double-taxation trap.
