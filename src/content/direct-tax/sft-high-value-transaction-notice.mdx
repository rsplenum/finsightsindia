When you buy a ₹50 Lakh SUV or park ₹10 Lakhs in an index fund, you might feel a sense of financial arrival. The Income Tax Department feels something else entirely: **suspicion**. 

The days of manual audits and random scrutiny are dead. Today, your financial life is tracked by a relentless, automated surveillance grid known as the **Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT)**. 

If your PAN card is attached to a transaction, the grid knows. The only question is whether the math on your Income Tax Return aligns with the lifestyle you are actively transmitting to the government servers.

### The Tripwires: When Does the Grid Ping?

The law under Section 285BA explicitly mandates that specific financial institutions—banks, mutual fund houses, registrars, and car dealerships—must act as informants. They are legally required to report you if you cross predefined thresholds.

If you trigger any of the following tripwires, your PAN is flagged and pushed to the Annual Information Statement (AIS) database:

- **The Cash Deposit:** Depositing ₹10 Lakhs or more in cash into a savings account (or ₹50 Lakhs into a current account).
- **The Equity Play:** Investing ₹10 Lakhs or more in shares, mutual funds, or bonds in a single financial year.
- **The Luxury Buy:** Purchasing any motor vehicle valued over ₹10 Lakhs.
- **The Real Estate Move:** Buying or selling immovable property valued at ₹30 Lakhs or more.
- **The Credit Card Swipe:** Paying credit card bills exceeding ₹1 Lakh in cash, or ₹10 Lakhs via any mode (NEFT/UPI).
- **The Forex Holiday:** Spending ₹10 Lakhs or more on foreign currency or overseas travel.

### The Algorithmic Match

The IT Department uses Big Data analytics (Project Insight) to construct a 360-degree profile of your wealth. They do not care about a single transaction in isolation. They care about the *ratio*. 

If your FDs, stock buys, and credit card spends far outpace your declared income, you are categorized as a high-risk profile. 

### How to Survive the Grid

You cannot hide from the SFT grid, but you can navigate it cleanly:

1. **Check Your AIS Before Filing:** Never file an ITR without downloading your Annual Information Statement. It shows you exactly what the government already knows about your spending.
2. **Reconcile Family Money:** If your parents gifted you the money to buy that ₹15 Lakh car, ensure they file an ITR that supports that gift, and draft a formal gift deed. The algorithm will follow the money trail back to them.
3. **Declare Exempt Income:** Even if income is tax-free (like agricultural income or certain bonds), declare it in the Exempt Income schedule. It provides the mathematical justification for your lifestyle.

### The Interrogation Room (Forensic Edge Cases)

**"I bought a car worth ₹12 Lakhs using a loan. Will I still be flagged?"**
Yes. The car dealership reports the *transaction value*, regardless of whether it was funded by a bank loan or cash. However, if an audit is triggered, your bank loan statement provides a complete, mathematically sound defense. The algorithm flags it; your documentation kills the notice.

**"I broke a ₹15 Lakh FD and reinvested it in Mutual Funds. Is that an SFT flag?"**
Yes, that’s actually *two* flags. One for the FD withdrawal and one for the mutual fund purchase. The algorithm will see ₹30 Lakhs of velocity. You must ensure your ITR clearly shows that the source of the mutual fund investment was the liquidated capital from the FD, not fresh un-taxed income.
