## The Breadcrumb Trail: Realized vs. Unrealized

The Income Tax Department is blind to your portfolio's daily fluctuations. They do not care if your portfolio is down 90% or up 500%. 

They only care about one word: **Realized**.

A profit or a loss does not legally exist in the eyes of the government until the asset is sold. If you hold a stock that is down ₹4,00,000, that is an *unrealized* loss. It provides you zero tax benefit. It is just dead weight.

But the moment you sell that stock, it becomes a **Realized Loss**. Under **Section 70** of the Income Tax Act, the government allows you to subtract realized losses from realized gains before calculating the final tax.

## The Defense: 360° Edge Cases

How exactly do you execute this without messing up? Let's explore the friction points.

### Edge Case 1: The "Wash Sale" Loophole
*"But I believe in the losing stock! I don't want to get rid of it permanently!"*
**The Shield:** In the United States, if you sell a stock for a loss and buy it back within 30 days, the IRS disallows the tax loss (the "Wash Sale Rule"). **India does not have a Wash Sale Rule.**
You can sell your losing HDFC shares on Monday at 10:00 AM to book the loss, and buy them back at 10:05 AM. You have successfully booked the tax loss, but your portfolio composition hasn't changed. (Just remember that your new buying price resets your holding period).

### Edge Case 2: The Caste System of Losses (Section 74)
You cannot mix and match losses randomly. The law has a strict hierarchy:
- **Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL):** The ultimate weapon. It can be used to offset *both* Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).
- **Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL):** The restricted weapon. It can *only* be used to offset Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). It cannot offset STCG.

### Edge Case 3: Intraday and F&O
*"Can I use my F&O losses to offset my stock market gains?"*
**The Reality:** No. F&O and Intraday trading are considered "Business Income." You cannot offset business losses against Capital Gains (Section 71). Tax loss harvesting only works by matching Capital Assets (delivery equity, mutual funds) against other Capital Assets.

### Edge Case 4: The 8-Year Carry Forward
What if your realized losses are *bigger* than your realized gains? What if you lost ₹10 Lakhs this year but only made ₹2 Lakhs?
**The Shield:** Under Section 74, if you file your ITR before the July deadline, you are legally permitted to carry forward the remaining ₹8 Lakh loss for the next **8 Financial Years**. You can use it to offset profits in any of those future years. But if you miss the ITR deadline, you lose the right to carry it forward.

## The Ultimate Fix (How to Execute)

You do not need an expensive CA to execute this. You just need 15 minutes and a calculator.

1. **Open Your Dashboard (Mid-March):** Go to your broker's "Tax P&L" report.
2. **Identify the Targets:** Look at your Unrealized P&L. Find the stocks or mutual funds sitting in deep red.
3. **Execute the Sell:** Sell enough of the losing assets to match the profit you want to offset.
4. **Execute the Buy (Optional):** If you want to keep the asset long-term, buy it back on the same day or the next day. 
5. **File on Time:** Ensure you file your ITR-2 or ITR-3 before July 31st to officially claim the set-off and carry forward any remaining losses.

By treating your portfolio as a dynamic tax shield rather than a static vault, you legally force the government to share in your downside, while protecting your upside.
